The problem

Incentives reward owning a clean vehicle, not making a clean journey

  • A commuter who takes the metro every day earns nothing, while an EV owner who takes a petrol cab for a day is treated as clean.
  • There is no cheap, verifiable record of what a single journey actually saved.
  • Without that record, an employer, a city or a sponsor cannot pay for behaviour change with confidence.

How it works

The server signs; the commuter's wallet submits

  • The verifier node recomputes distance and carbon itself and signs only what it derived.
  • The contract pays msg.sender, so the oracle can sign a trip but can never submit it.
  • Streak and carbon score live on-chain.
  • Petrol, diesel and simulated trips are never signed.

Why Portaldot V3

EVM compatibility is what makes this practical

  • Standard tools just work: EIP-712 typed-data signatures, ecrecover and an ERC-20 token, with ordinary wallets.
  • Commuter-submitted: users keep control; the oracle needs no funds and no custody.
  • An open record: balances, streaks, score and processed trips are public.
  • Honest scope: we use the EVM layer. We do not claim WASM or cross-chain features.

Business potential

Who pays, and how we will test it

Employers and campuses

A programme fee per active commuter, with an emissions report

Operators and cities

Funded reward pools and evidence of mode shift on a corridor

Sponsors

Reward pools tied to a measured outcome, not an impression

  • First users: one corridor or campus in Delhi NCR, where the grid factor and transit modes are already modelled.
  • Before a public pilot: Sign-In with Ethereum, a database for the oracle's limits, sourced metro and e-bus factors.
  • We name our risks: GPS spoofing, $MEV is not a carbon credit, factor quality, a single oracle key, transaction cost.
